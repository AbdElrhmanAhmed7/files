--- THE NOVEL AT A GLANCE ---
- Writer: Arthur Conan Doyle (1859-1930), famous British writer and doctor; creator of Sherlock Holmes; also wrote "The Lost World" and many other stories and novels.
- Setting — Place: begins in London, England (the Daily Gazette where Malone works, the Savage Club, Professor Challenger's large house in Kensington), then moves to South America — the Amazon, and the Lost World plateau ("Maple White Land").
- Setting — Time: the early 20th century. Atmosphere: adventurous, mysterious, dangerous and exciting.
- Plot: Edward Malone joins a dangerous expedition to South America with Professor Challenger and other explorers to discover prehistoric animals; they face dangers and mysteries in the Lost World and try to survive and find the truth.
- Main characters:
  * Professor Challenger — brilliant, confident, aggressive zoologist who claims prehistoric animals still exist and leads the expedition.
  * Edward Malone — young, ambitious Daily Gazette reporter who seeks adventure to prove himself and give his life purpose.
  * Professor Summerlee — intelligent, serious, sceptical scientist who values evidence and accepts the truth only after seeing it himself.
  * Lord John Roxton — brave, experienced explorer, sportsman and hunter, familiar with the Amazon, who protects the team.
- Secondary characters: Gladys Hungerton (the woman Malone loves); Mr McArdle (experienced news editor); Tarp Henry (Malone's friend at the Savage Club); Mrs Challenger (kind wife who warns Malone about her husband's temper); Austin (quiet servant); Maple White (dying American explorer whose sketchbook starts it all); Zambo (strong, loyal helper below the cliffs); Gomez (the betrayer); Manuel and the Indigenous boatmen; Pedro Lopez (mentioned — Gomez's brother, dead leader of an unfair rubber-trading group).
- Main themes: courage and proving oneself; respect evidence before accepting extraordinary ideas; curiosity and the desire for discovery; determination; do not judge people before knowing the truth; bravery in the face of danger; teamwork overcomes difficult challenges.

--- CHAPTER 1: A DREAM OF ADVENTURE ---
Summary:
- Young reporter Edward Malone, longing for adventure and a greater purpose, proposes to Gladys Hungerton; she refuses, saying she could only love a brave man who has faced danger, travelled into the unknown, and proved his courage.
- Malone asks his editor Mr. McArdle for a dangerous assignment; McArdle points him to Professor Challenger, recently back from South America with a strange story of unknown animals and refusing to give clear details — mistaken or lying, "find out which."
- With Tarp Henry's help, Malone writes a trick letter posing as an admiring student and quoting a sentence from Challenger's own scientific paper; Challenger replies and grants an appointment.
- At Challenger's large house in Kensington, Mrs. Challenger warns Malone about her husband's famous temper.
- A deep roar comes from behind the closed study door, and Malone enters to face the broad, black-bearded professor behind a desk covered with books, maps and fossils — his adventure has truly begun.

--- CHAPTER 2: PROFESSOR CHALLENGER'S SECRET ---
Summary:
- Challenger's technical questions quickly expose Malone's ignorance; Malone admits he is a Daily Gazette reporter; furious, Challenger throws him out, and the two fall fighting together onto the front steps before a policeman and neighbours.
- Malone refuses to press charges, admitting the fault was partly his own; Challenger's anger turns into something like respect, and he invites Malone back inside.
- After Malone promises not to publish anything without permission, Challenger shows Maple White's worn sketchbook (red cliffs, an isolated rock tower, a stegosaurus-like animal), a damaged photograph, and a pterodactyl wing bone.
- Challenger explains his theory that the high cliffs of an isolated plateau cut it off from the rest of the world, so prehistoric animals could have survived there; he refuses to reveal the exact location because people mocked him and accused him of faking evidence.
- Malone moves from scepticism to growing belief; Challenger tells him of a public lecture at the Zoological Institute the next evening, and Malone leaves imagining a hidden world beyond the Atlantic.

--- CHAPTER 3: THE EXPEDITION IS CHOSEN ---
Summary:
- At a crowded public lecture at the Zoological Institute, Challenger angrily defends the possibility that prehistoric creatures survive; Professor Summerlee challenges him to provide testable proof and volunteers to test it himself.
- Malone and the famous explorer Lord John Roxton volunteer as younger companions; Challenger proposes Summerlee, Roxton and Malone as the investigating party, and the motion is accepted.
- Roxton tells Malone about his feud with Pedro Lopez's unfair rubber-trading group, and tests Malone's shooting, climbing, swimming and calm under pressure; the team prepares and sails to Brazil, hiring Zambo, Gomez, Manuel and Indigenous boatmen.
- Malone visits Gladys before leaving but does not say he is traveling for her.
- Challenger's sealed instructions, to be opened at exactly noon on 15 July in Manaos, contain only a blank sheet of paper — Challenger has secretly traveled to Brazil to guide the expedition himself, and the four men set off for the unknown.

--- CHAPTER 4: INTO THE UNKNOWN ---
Summary:
- The steam launch Esmeralda is sent back as the Amazon tributary narrows; the group continues by canoe, and later on foot carrying supplies, while Challenger makes all promise never to publish enough information for strangers to locate the plateau.
