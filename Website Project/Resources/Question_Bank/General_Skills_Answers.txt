     Can Viral History Be Trusted?
     No — viral history cannot be trusted on its own, and the last few years of "historical" posts prove it. Viral content is optimized for emotion, not accuracy: a dramatic claim ("the photo the authorities hid") travels faster than any correction, and platforms reward whatever keeps viewers scrolling. The recent cases are sobering — a "1919 Revolution" photograph that archives traced to a different event entirely, a popular video about an ancient object whose date the museum itself had to correct, and a page reposting an old tram photo stripped of all context. In each case, millions saw the error; only thousands saw the correction. Trust, therefore, belongs not in virality but in verification: does the post name its sources, do recognized archives or museums confirm it, and does honest context accompany the image? A historical claim without sources is storytelling — and often propaganda. My rule: enjoy viral history as a trailer, then check the film in the archives. Virality measures speed, never truth — and history deserves better than speed.
